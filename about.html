Since 1998

A small building on Sydney Street, and one stubborn idea

That a garment is worth more than the sum of its cleaning. Twenty-seven years later there are thirty-one of us, nine benches, and the same refusal to put a cashmere coat through a cycle designed for a raincoat.

The story

It started as a repair shop

Renata Blanc trained as a tailor in Milan and came to London in 1994 to alter other people's suits. Four years later she took a lease on Sydney Street with the intention of doing repairs, and found that most of what arrived did not need mending. It needed cleaning properly.

So she bought a second-hand spotting board, learned the chemistry, and started turning away work she could not do well — a habit the house has never lost. The first hydrocarbon machine arrived in 2006. The last drop of perchloroethylene left the building in 2016.

Renata still comes in on Tuesdays. She still checks the bridal bench herself.

How we clean

Nine benches, in order

Nothing here is automated except the machines that need to be. A garment passes through at least four pairs of hands between your door and your rail.

01 · Intake

Every piece photographed, tagged with a woven marker that survives cleaning, and logged against your account with its fibre content and any existing damage.

02 · Assessment

Fibre, construction, dye stability, trim, lining and the actual nature of each mark. This is the step that decides everything after it, and it is never rushed.

03 · Spotting

Fourteen reagents, applied by hand under a vacuum board. Protein, tannin, pigment and oil are four different problems with four different answers.

04 · Cleaning

Closed-loop hydrocarbon for solvent work, and professional wet cleaning for everything that will take water — which is more than most people assume.

05 · Finishing

Hand pressing on shaped forms by pressers trained on Savile Row. Machines flatten. People shape. The difference is visible at two metres.

06 · Final check

A second inspection against the intake photograph. If it is not right it goes back down the line, and you are told why it is a day late.

Sustainability

The least bad way to do this

Dry cleaning has a poor environmental record and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Here is exactly what we have changed, and what we have not yet solved.

  • No perchloroethylene since 2016Replaced with hydrocarbon in a closed-loop machine that recovers 99.5% of solvent.
  • 62% of garments wet cleanedWater and biodegradable detergent, wherever the fibre allows it.
  • No single-use plastic since 2019Reusable garment bags collected back on your next order. We have taken 190,000 polythene covers out of circulation.
  • Hangers taken back and reusedWooden ones refurbished, wire ones returned to the manufacturer.
  • Still unsolved: the vansFour of our seven are electric. The other three are diesel and will be replaced by 2027, not sooner, because we are not going to pretend a lease ends early.
