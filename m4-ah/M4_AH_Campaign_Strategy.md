## Alex’s story rules and how to use the copy
Eight slides: Open, Hold, four Spine slides, Turn, Payoff. Cover ≤3 words; other slides ≤40 visible words, including any evidence label. The first-month scripts reserve words for “Illustrative example”; word counts include it. Visible text is separate from art instructions.

The first-month middle follows a specific dependency: circumstance → unresolved information → consequence → correction → test. The Turn states what that sequence now allows the owner to see or do. The Payoff establishes a belief and one action. Slides should be independently understandable without becoming interchangeable advice cards.

Eight topic-specific cover options are ranked editorially for the first month. The selected option is written to match the Hold. Selecting another cover requires checking and, where necessary, rewriting slide two. Later drafts have a working cover and development requirements rather than eight generic filler alternatives.

Variation should include scenes, annotated messages, diary comparisons, process states, capability matrices and evidence cards. Avoid identical typography or a rigid visual alternation. SHORT/DENSE denotes visual hierarchy rather than an extra label on the finished post.

Alex’s slide-two likes benchmark is his own heuristic, not established swipe retention. Do not call a like-location ratio the percentage of viewers who continued. Use account metrics actually available; no numerical carousel-retention target is imposed here.

Track the secondary website demo and supporting receptionist demo separately. Define a qualified enquiry as a relevant salon business with an identifiable need and interest in a next step. Keep this agency funnel separate from illustrative salon client enquiries, appointments and attendance.

Reach and Reel views are not interchangeable denominators.

Establish the actual baseline, then choose numeric targets. Keep the historical 2,500 follower goal visible without inventing progress.
