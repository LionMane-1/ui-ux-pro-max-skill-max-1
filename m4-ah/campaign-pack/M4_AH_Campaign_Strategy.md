Prepared 6 October 2026. Proposed start: Monday 12 October 2026. End: Sunday 11 April 2027. Exactly 26 weeks / 182 days, covering six calendar months. Shift dates if launch readiness requires it.

## The recommendation
Use Alex’s eight-slide story as the daily anchor available in the content bank. The daily ceiling is one carousel, one 15–25-second Reel and one three-frame Story sequence. Three Story frames count as one sequence, not three campaign posts. A daily theme produces three complementary treatments: explain, demonstrate, converse.

Begin with three carousels (Monday, Wednesday, Friday), two Reels (Tuesday, Thursday), and one Story sequence daily: 12 publishing units weekly. The workbook marks these Core; the remaining nine weekly units are Optional. That gives 312 core units and 234 optional units, or 546 at maximum cadence. Sunday should be a light review, not an extra sales push. All scripts are supplied so reduced frequency does not create a production gap.

The bank is written copy and visual briefs, not finished graphics or rendered videos. Its recurring story scaffold supports production, but vary the visual treatment and specificity; do not publish mechanically. Seven related days form one weekly story rather than seven unrelated lists.

## Strategic basis
The supplied May customer-avatar document identifies problem-aware salon owners as the principal acquisition audience. Unaware owners need recognition without blame; solution-aware owners need systems and comparisons; product-aware owners need scope and evidence; ready buyers need a clear route. Avatar descriptions, income ranges and willingness-to-pay figures in that document are planning assumptions, not market research.

Lead with missed enquiries, weak local discovery, unused reviews and booking friction. Use Instagram as the initial carousel channel; adapt selected assets for Facebook. Reels can be adapted for TikTok and YouTube Shorts; these are distribution copies, not additional original campaigns. Keep messaging addressed to salon owners, not consumers seeking a hairstyle.

Conversion path: relevant content → Revenue Leak Calculator → salon growth-system explanation → package information → verified onboarding route. This is the previously established strategy, not a claim these assets are already live. Offer ladder: Growth Foundation, Operational Growth, Platinum Scale. These are positioning categories; current prices, inclusions and terms must be confirmed before publication. Do not import the group’s SaaS prices or the unrelated junk-removal trial.

The old July follower target is past. Establish current followers and lead performance at launch. For the first month, success is a functioning funnel, a measured baseline and evidence of useful salon-owner enquiries. Set commercial targets from those data rather than inventing conversion benchmarks.

## Evidence and trust rules
Label every simulated conversation, illustrative scenario and mock interface on the published asset. Replace them with authorised live examples where useful. Do not present Keisha as a real satisfied customer. Do not manufacture results, rankings, reviews, availability, guarantees or package prices. Avoid diagnosing hair or scalp conditions in marketing copy; service preparation and aftercare need stylist approval.

Google permits neutral invitations for genuine reviews. It prohibits incentives and selective solicitation of positive reviews. Use the same invitation regardless of satisfaction and do not script review keywords. Sources checked 6 October 2026: https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122 and https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114 . The reviews material is operational guidance, not a substitute for checking policies again at publication.

Promotional follow-up is conditional on the applicable channel permissions and rules. This campaign does not assert that a particular contact can lawfully receive marketing. Client-facing reply examples should be reviewed against the actual salon’s services, policies and permissions.

## Measurement and decisions
Record post ID, date, format, topic, awareness stage, CTA, reach/views, saves, shares, profile visits where available, destination visits, qualified salon-owner enquiries, booked consultations, attended consultations and resulting customers. A qualified enquiry identifies a relevant salon, a service problem and interest in a next step. Keep client appointment metrics within salon demonstrations separate from M4 AH’s agency sales funnel.

Save rate = saves / reached accounts. Share rate = shares / reached accounts. Qualified-enquiry rate = attributed qualified enquiries / tracked destination visits, where attribution exists. Consultation attendance = attended / booked consultations. Customer conversion = new paying salon customers / qualified enquiries for a consistently defined cohort. Empty denominators are blank, not zero. Deduplicate people and note unattributed enquiries. These describe observed associations, not proof of causation.

Use campaign identifiers such as m4ah_w01_d01_carousel. Add UTM parameters only to verified destination URLs; store source and post ID in the enquiry form where feasible. Compare each post after seven days, then review customer outcomes over an agreed longer window. Keep format definitions consistent; Reel views and carousel reach are not equivalent denominators.

First 14 days: check execution, links and whether the intended audience responds. End of week 4: record the baseline and set numeric commercial targets. Weeks 5–8: test the optional second daily feed post on selected days, changing only cadence where possible. Increase toward the maximum only if weekly qualified enquiries improve without deteriorating enquiry quality or missed follow-through. This is an internal decision rule, not a platform benchmark. If results are inconclusive, retain the sustainable core cadence.

Test one variable per comparison: cover, CTA or format. For cover tests, keep the core story and destination the same, compare equivalent post-age windows, and recognise that organic tests are not randomised experiments. Never imply guaranteed significance from a small sample.
